Why certification matters

Environmental testing is only as good as the people and the lab behind it.

  • Your permit and your project depend on it

    Oregon DEQ and Southwest Washington air agencies expect asbestos surveys from accredited inspectors and listed labs. A report from an uncertified tester can stall a permit or a sale.

  • Accurate results need trained hands

    Most bad results come from bad sampling, like a DIY kit that grabs only the top layer of a floor. Certified inspectors know which layers to sample and how to do it without spreading fibers.

  • Independence protects you

    We specialize only in testing and never sell abatement or removal, so we have no financial interest in “finding” asbestos. Oregon and Washington require clearance testing from a different company than the one that did the abatement.

  • A paper trail that holds up

    A certified report is documentation you can hand to a buyer, tenant, insurer or inspector. It protects you during a home sale, a renovation or a dispute.

Learn before you test

Plain-English guides to what’s in older buildings

Asbestos and lead paint were common building materials for most of the twentieth century, and mold is very common in the Pacific Northwest.

Asbestos 101

Why you can’t tell by looking

Where it hides
Popcorn ceilings, vinyl floor tiles (especially 9″×9″) and mastic, pipe and duct insulation, siding and roofing, and drywall joint compound.
When it’s a risk
When crumbly (“friable”) material is disturbed and fibers get into the air, for example by sanding, cutting or demolition.
Who should test
Anyone about to remodel or demolish, or buying a home. In the Portland and Vancouver area, homes that haven’t been fully renovated very likely contain some.
Lead 101

Old paint, real risk

Where it hides
Homes built before 1978 may have lead paint, and it’s much more likely before 1950: windows and sills, doors, stairs and railings, porches, fences and exterior walls.
Why it matters
Lead dust from chipping or sanded paint is invisible and especially harmful to children.
How we test
A SciAps X-550 XRF reads a surface in 2–6 seconds without damaging it. Paint chips are analyzed in the lab by XRF and FAAS.
Mold & Air 101

What you breathe at home

Signs to test
Musty smells, past leaks, or unexplained stuffiness, coughing, wheezing or eye irritation.
How we test
Air sampling measures airborne spores. Tape-lift sampling identifies what’s growing on a surface. Our package: indoor air, outdoor air and a furnace tape-lift.
Beyond mold
Indoor air quality testing for dust mites, dander, pollen and fibers, and mildew.

FAQ

Common questions about testing

How fast can I get results?

Choose Rush (same day), Next Day, 2-Day or 5-Day. For same-day rush, we need to receive your sample by 2 PM that day. Lead paint chips have a flat one-to-two-business-day turnaround. Faster turnaround costs more.

Can I drop off a sample after hours?

Yes. Each of our three labs has an after-hours drop box, and there are four partner drop boxes in Portland, St. Helens and Longview. For same-day rush, the sample has to reach us by 2 PM. Partner drop boxes are picked up on set schedules (Aramsco in Portland: daily at 10am).

How fast are lead paint results?

On site, our SciAps X-550 XRF reads a painted surface in 2 to 6 seconds without damaging it, so you get readings during the inspection. Paint chips you bring in have a flat one-to-two-business-day turnaround.

How do I bring in a sample?

Put the material in a sealable sandwich bag. A piece from thumbnail size up to about 2″ × 2″ is plenty (about 5 grams for lead). Take it from a spot that includes every layer that will be disturbed. Fill out the chain of custody form and pick your turnaround, then drop it at any of our three labs or four drop boxes.

Do I need an asbestos survey before I renovate?

Usually, yes. In Oregon, DEQ requires an asbestos survey before demolition or renovation of all commercial buildings and of homes built before 2004. In Washington, a survey is required before work that could disturb asbestos in any building, regardless of age. Owner-occupants of single-family homes may have some exemptions, so ask us what applies to your project.

How much does testing cost?

It depends on the number of samples, the size of the area and how fast you need results. We charge per sample, not per layer: one sample fee covers up to four layers. Call us or send the form for a quote.

If you find asbestos, can you remove it?

No. We are strictly a testing firm and do not offer abatement or removal. That keeps our results objective: we have no financial interest in finding a problem. We can do the independent clearance test after an abatement contractor finishes, and we schedule it as soon as the work is complete to minimize delays.

Can the abatement company do its own clearance test?

No. Oregon and Washington rules require the clearance assessment to be done by a different company than the one that did the abatement, so the results are unbiased.

Can I pay at a drop box?

No. Payment is taken at an Atlas Labs location or by phone, not at the drop boxes.

Is a DIY test kit good enough?

DIY kits often miss layers, which can lead to false results. Most permit-related surveys require a certified professional, and a professional report gives you a paper trail for a sale or renovation.
